### What the page is for

A PM opens this page for one of five reasons, in a fixed order of
urgency. Each is a screen. Nothing else is on that screen.

1. **Is it running?** Broker, gateway, last session (armed or not, and
   why not), data freshness, alerts, the next session's time.
2. **Is it real?** Which sessions have broker-confirmed fills, which are
   modelled; realised cost vs the 32.6bp breakeven; realised turnover vs
   the pre-registered 17.6×/yr; the NAV line broken at the first
   unconfirmed session. This book has armed on 3 of 26 sessions and 22 of
   24 ledger dates were modelled until the epoch reset; the second
   question exists because of that history.
3. **What do we hold and what is the risk?** Names and groups, gross,
   net, effective breadth, the muni-vs-taxable share of variance, net muni
   weight, borrow drag, utilisation of each short's pool, the signal
   columns (z, discount, target, gap) as columns of the same table.
4. **What trades tonight?** The order list the executor will send, and
   for the names that will not trade, how far each is from its band edge.
5. **What did it cost?** Fill-level shortfall, session series, running
   mean against breakeven, the impact test.

Benchmarks (empty until every reference book has two NAV rows), the
independent re-derivation checks, the risk limits table, and doctor's
plumbing rows are real but secondary; they move into a "More" drawer
reachable from screen 1's status line, and each surfaces on screen 1 only
when it is not green.

### What to cut, and why

- The **Book** tab today is eight "n/a" tiles and a banner explaining the
  n/a. Eight identical reasons are one sentence. The tab becomes screen 2.
- The **Signal** tab duplicates the Positions table with different
  columns. Merge: the blotter (P3.1) carries z, discount, target weight,
  gap, and the valuation-dispersion bar chart sits above it as the only
  chart on screen 3.
- The **Benchmarks** tab has one plotted series. Until three books have
  ≥ 20 sessions it is a single row in the drawer: "Benchmarks: 0 of 5
  comparable (need ≥ 2 NAV rows each)".
- The **Controls** tab becomes the drawer: pre-trade checks, risk limits,
  doctor. Its chip lives on screen 1.
- Four masthead numbers (book NAV, gross, net, names) are the same
  numbers screen 3 shows; keep NAV and as-of in the masthead, drop the
  rest.

### Rules the redesign inherits

- Every value that cannot be computed gets `nodata(title, reason)` with a
  reason from the server. Keep that function; it is the most important
  component on the page. But **one gap, one sentence**: when a whole card
  is unavailable for one reason, render one block, not one per tile.
- Provenance is one fact per ledger; it is one chip on screen 2's NAV
  card and one word ("modelled") wherever a modelled number appears.
- Colour carries a verdict or nothing. Status (good/warn/crit), the
  cheap/rich poles for the signal, up/down for P&L. No decorative colour.
- Every panel degrades independently (`step()` stays).
- Tabs are deep-linkable (`#running`, `#real`, `#book`, `#trades`,
  `#cost`) and the last tab is remembered.
- Charts measure their container; redraw on tab show and on resize.

### Acceptance

- With the gateway down, every screen still renders, screen 1 is red with
  the reason, screen 3 shows the last snapshot greyed with its timestamp.
- Time to first paint of screen 1 under 500ms on a warm server.
- No tab shows more than one chart. No screen shows more than one table
  plus one card row.
- A new team member can say what each screen is for from its heading
  alone.

### Do not

- Do not add a framework, a bundler, or a chart library.
- Do not add a second POST route.
- Do not remove any server-side reason string; move them, do not drop
  them.
