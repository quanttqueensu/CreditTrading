### The mechanism

A closed-end fund's NAV is the fund accountant's valuation of the
portfolio. For a high-yield bond fund most holdings have a TRACE print
that day; the NAV is fresh. For a municipal fund most holdings are
matrix-priced from a pricing service that moves evaluated prices
gradually; the NAV is a smoothed version of true value. For a loan fund
the marks are dealer quotes that update in steps. So the published NAV
$N^o_t$ relates to the true value $N_t$ approximately as a moving average
of recent true changes (Getmansky, Lo & Makarov 2004):

$$r^o_t = \sum_{j=0}^{q} \theta_j\, r_{t-j},\qquad \sum_j \theta_j = 1,\ \theta_j \ge 0,$$

where $r$ are log NAV returns. The observed discount
$d^o = P/N^o - 1$ then contains a component that is **NAV lag, not price
dislocation**: after a rally in the underlying bonds, a muni CEF's NAV
has not yet caught up, so the fund looks rich and the signal shorts it;
the NAV then catches up and the "convergence" is the NAV moving, which
earns the fund nothing.

The prediction is specific and falsifiable: unsmoothing should improve
the IC in muni and loan names, do little in HY names, and the NAV
autocorrelation should be highest in munis before and near zero after.

### The construction, no lookahead

Per name, on a trailing 504-session window ending the day before $t$,
refit monthly:

1. Estimate the MA($q$) smoothing profile $\theta$ on log NAV returns
   with $q$ fixed at 2 (GLM's finding for illiquid hedge-fund series;
   report $q$ = 1 and 3 as sensitivity but do not select on them). Use
   maximum likelihood on the MA representation or the GLM moment
   estimator; normalise $\sum\theta = 1$.
2. Invert: $\hat r_t = \left(r^o_t - \sum_{j=1}^{q}\theta_j \hat r_{t-j}\right)/\theta_0$.
3. Reconstruct a level. Inverting returns and cumulating drifts; anchor
   the unsmoothed log level to the observed log level's trailing 21-day
   mean (a trailing anchor uses no future data). Report the mean absolute
   level difference by group; it should be largest in munis.
4. $d^u_t = 100\,(P_t - \hat N_t)/\hat N_t$; the z-score on $d^u$ with the
   **identical** 252-day moments, shift 1, clip ±4.

Alignment assertions: $\theta$ for date $t$ fitted on data ≤ $t-1$; the
anchor window ends at $t-1$.

**Group-specific alternative, to run alongside as the second variant:**
a NAV **nowcast** from a live-priced proxy. For each group pick one ETF
whose return proxies the underlying bonds (muni: a muni ETF, fetched into
the panel if absent; HY: HYG from `data/rv/etf_ohlc.parquet`; loan: BKLN
or SRLN; multi: a duration-matched blend of HYG and LQD). Regress the
fund's NAV return on the proxy's contemporaneous and lagged returns over
the trailing 504 sessions; the nowcast NAV change is the fitted
contemporaneous part plus the *already-observed* proxy moves the NAV has
not yet reflected (the sum of significant lag coefficients times recent
proxy returns). This is M2 by a different route and it is where the
group structure enters explicitly.

### The tests

1. **Diagnostics first.** NAV return autocorrelation at lags 1–3 by name

2. **IC by group**, 2-day/T+1, non-overlapping, 2005–2026 and by era, for

muni/loan; the 27 agree in sign; and the harness shows net@15 and net@30
