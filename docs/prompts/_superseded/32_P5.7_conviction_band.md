### Why this is the right place for per-name treatment

The z-score already carries each fund's $\kappa^{1/2}$ in its denominator
(the OU stationary variance argument in PLAN §4.1), which is why adding κ
to the score double-counted and lost. The *trading rule* carries nothing:
one width, one implied holding period, for a $4.50 fund with a 22bp tick
and a 56-day half-life and for a $15 fund with a 6.5bp tick and a 23-day
half-life, and for a name at |z| = 3 that will converge in days and one at
|z| = 0.7 that needs two months to clear the spread. The policy layer is
where per-name and per-signal information is absent, and policy derived
from measurement costs no trials.

### Two variants, one run

**Variant A, conviction (per signal):**
$$b_{i,t} = b_0 \left(\frac{h^*(|z_{i,t}|)}{\bar h}\right)^{1/3},$$
with $b_0$ = 4.8%, $\bar h$ = the live band's measured hold from the
harness (~12–16d; measure it), and $h^*(\cdot)$ the step function from the
exit study's table above (or its monotone smooth: fit an isotonic
decreasing function through the five points; state which). Because $h^*$
is 60d for |z| < 1.5 and 2–8d above, this widens the band on weak signals
(hold longer, trade less) and narrows it on strong ones (harvest and
release). $|z_{i,t}|$ is the current signal, so as a position converges
its band widens and it is left alone, which is the intended behaviour.
Clip $b_{i,t}$ to [1.0%, 12.8%], the evaluated range.

**Variant B, per-name structure (PER_NAME_ARCHITECTURE Phase A):**
$$b_i = \left(\frac{3\, c_i\, \sigma_{w,i}^2}{2\,\kappa_{w,i}}\right)^{1/3}$$
with $c_i$ the name's half-spread (0.005/price), $\sigma_{w,i}$ and
$\kappa_{w,i}$ the daily volatility and AR(1) reversion of the name's
*target weight* series (the same quantities used to derive 4.8% for the
pooled book, now per name; estimate on a trailing 504-session window
ending the day before, refit monthly, empirical-Bayes shrunk toward the
pooled values with the weights `per_name_resolution.py` computes; report
raw and shrunk). Clip as above.

**Variant C, both:** A's factor applied to B's base.
