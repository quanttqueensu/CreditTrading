### The hypothesis

A Ledoit-Wolf estimate on 17 names and 252 days, refit monthly, moves
from month to month for reasons that are estimation noise, and the
optimiser trades on those moves. That is the plausible source of the
joint book's turnover instability. A **structured** covariance with the
two factors we already know exist (market, muni-vs-taxable) has far fewer
free parameters (2×17 loadings + 3 factor covariances + 17 residuals = 54,
against 153 in the sample covariance), a well-conditioned inverse by
construction (Woodbury), and month-to-month changes that come from
loadings drifting, which is slower.

If the hypothesis is right, the joint optimiser with structured Σ keeps
its gross and net advantage and loses most of its turnover instability.
If it is wrong, the instability is coming from the alpha, not Σ, and
that is worth knowing before P5.3.

### The construction

Per estimation date $t$ (first session of each month), on the trailing
252 complete-case return rows ending the day **before** $t$:

- Factor 1: $f^{(1)}_s$ = equal-weight mean return of the eligible
  universe on day $s$.
- Factor 2: $f^{(2)}_s$ = mean return of the muni names minus mean return
  of the taxable names (groups from `data/cef/cef_universe.csv`; the
  loan and hy names are taxable). Orthogonalise $f^{(2)}$ against
  $f^{(1)}$ by regression so the loadings are interpretable; report both
  raw and orthogonalised results.
- Loadings $B$ (17×2) by OLS of each name's return on the two factors
  over the window; residuals $\varepsilon$.
- $F$ = 2×2 sample covariance of the factors; $D$ = diag of residual
  variances.
- $\Sigma_{struct} = B F B' + D$.
- Third variant, **shrinkage toward the structured target**:
  $\Sigma_{LW\to struct} = \delta\,\Sigma_{struct} + (1-\delta)\,S$ with
  $S$ the sample covariance and $\delta$ from the Ledoit-Wolf closed form
  for a general target (Ledoit & Wolf 2003, "Improved estimation … with
  an application to portfolio selection" gives the single-factor case;
  implement the general-target intensity or use `sklearn`'s LW intensity
  applied to the residual after projecting out the factor part; state
  which).

Alignment: every window ends at $t-1$; assert it in code.

### The comparison

Inside `joint_cost_optimiser.py`'s loop, holding everything else fixed
(signal, α units, λ calibration, $c_{model}$, ADV closure, refit

|---|---|---|
| Ledoit-Wolf (current) | | |
| sample (unshrunk; the known-bad control) | | |
| structured, two factors | | |

For each, turnover-matched to the band at 14.2/yr **and** at the joint's
own natural turnover:

| Σ | turn/yr | gross | net@5 | net@15 | net@30 | BR_eff | PC2 share | era sd/mean of turnover | 2013–16 net@15 | mean names traded/session | autocorr of the traded set |

The last column: Jaccard similarity of the set of names with $d_i \ne 0$
between consecutive sessions; a stable no-trade set is what we want.

Also plot (matplotlib, saved to `results/cef/figures/`) the time series
of $\Sigma$'s Frobenius change between refits for each variant, and
BR_eff over time.

### Decision rule

- The structured variants are adopted into the joint construction (P1.7)
  if turnover sd/mean falls below 20% (roughly halfway to the band's
  11.9%) while net@15 at matched turnover is within 0.05 of LW's and the
  2013–16 era no longer loses to the band.
- If the sample covariance's instability and the structured variant's
  are similar, Σ is not the source; write that, and P5.3 becomes the
  route.
- Nothing is swept: two factors, one window, one refit cadence, all
  fixed in advance.
