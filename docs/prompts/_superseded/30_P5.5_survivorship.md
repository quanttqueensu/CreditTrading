### Why the direction of the bias is not obvious here

The naive story: dead funds performed badly, so a survivor-only panel
overstates returns. For a long-only fund that is right. For this book it
is subtler:

- The long leg buys funds at unusually wide discounts. Funds that
  eventually liquidate or merge spend their last years at wide discounts
  (small, illiquid, cutting distributions). A survivor-only panel removes
  exactly the names the long leg would have been buying, and their exit
  outcomes: a merger or open-ending at NAV is a **windfall** for a long
  (the discount closes to zero on a date); a liquidation at a fire-sale
  price is a loss; a fund that simply drifted wider for years is a
  long-leg loser.
- The short leg shorts rich funds. Rich funds rarely die.

So the missing names would have been disproportionately on the long leg,
and their exits were bimodal. The bias could plausibly be in either
direction and the only way to know is to find them.

- SEC EDGAR: N-CEN (2018+) item B.2/B.3 for mergers and liquidations;

  AllianceBernstein, MFS, Morgan Stanley, Franklin/Templeton, Putnam,

- Fund-family press releases on completed mergers (Nuveen and BlackRock
  publish these; Nuveen consolidated dozens of muni CEFs 2014–2020).
- CEFConnect historical fund lists and the ICI's annual closed-end fund
  statistics for counts by year (to bound how many we are missing).
- yfinance: some delisted tickers still return history; try each dead
  ticker.

Aim for the credit universe: taxable bond, high-yield, loan, municipal,
emerging-market debt CEFs listed on NYSE/AMEX 1998–2026. Record how many
were found and estimate, from ICI counts by year, what fraction of the
dead population that is.

**2. Recover history where possible.** For each dead fund with
recoverable price and NAV (yfinance, EDGAR N-CSR NAV tables, CEFConnect

**3. Rerun the live specification on a point-in-time universe:** eligible
on date $t$ if listed, ADV clears the screen, and alive. Terminal returns

use the last price and flag it. Report gross, net@15, net of borrow,
turnover, by era, against the survivor-only panel, and the IC.

**4. Bound what could not be recovered.** For dead funds without price
history, two bounding scenarios applied to the long leg's typical weight
in a name of their size: (a) each earned the worst-quintile survivor
return over its final 252 days and exited at the last price; (b) each
exited at NAV (merger/open-ending). Report the Sharpe under each as the
range the true number sits in.

**5. Write it up** in `results/cef/SURVIVORSHIP_<date>.md` with the list,
the coverage estimate, the PIT rerun, and the bounds. Update
