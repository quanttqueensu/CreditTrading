# P3.2 — Per-name drill-down: how did this name get here

**Lever:** a trader's context for one position. **Trials:** 0. **Touches
the live book:** no.

---

## Paste from here

You are adding a per-name view to the QUANTT CEF dashboard, opened by
clicking a ticker anywhere on the page, routed as `#name/NAD`. Read
`dashboard/server.py::_signal_frame` (exactly how the discount, its
rolling mean and sd, and z are built: 252-day window, `min_periods` 120,
both moments `shift(1)`), `src/deploy/sleeves/cef_discount.py`,
`scripts/cef/per_name_resolution.py` (the measured per-name attributes
and the derived band table in `docs/PER_NAME_ARCHITECTURE.md` §4),
`data/cef/cef_borrow.csv`, and the CEF shadow ledger's `positions.csv`
and `broker_fills.csv`.

### What the view answers

"Why are we short NAD at 18.6% of NAV, how long have we been, what would
make the band move it, and what has it cost to hold?"

### Layout, top to bottom, all inline SVG

1. **Header:** ticker, name (from `data/cef/cef_facts.csv` `longName`),
   group, price, NAV, discount, z, side and held weight, target weight,
   distance to edge, fee %, utilisation. Same numbers as the blotter row.

2. **Discount chart, 500 sessions:** the discount (%), its 252-day rolling
   mean as a line, ±1 sd and ±2 sd as shaded bands. These are **exactly
   the quantities the z-score uses**, with the one-day shift applied, so
   the trader sees the signal's own frame of reference. Mark the last
   value. Mark distribution ex-dates as small ticks on the x-axis (the
   ex-date moves price and not NAV; a one-day sawtooth is not dislocation).

3. **Price vs NAV chart, same window:** two lines, log scale, so
   convergence reads as the lines meeting.

4. **Our position, same window:** held weight as a step line, with fills
   from `broker_fills.csv` marked as dots (buy above, sell below) sized by
   notional, and modelled-only sessions shaded, so the trader can see what
   was real.

5. **Borrow, when there is history:** fee % and pool shares from
   `cef_borrow.csv` as two small lines; "1 day of data" until there is
   more. Never interpolate.

6. **Measured attributes table** (from `per_name_resolution.py`, computed
   once per day and cached): half-life (bd), discount sd, price vol, tick
   bp, ADV, and the derived per-name band width next to the live 4.8%
   with the sentence from PER_NAME_ARCHITECTURE: "an expensive name is not
   a bad name, it is a slow one."

7. **Recent sessions table:** last 10 sessions for this name: date,
   decision, order, fill, fill vs decision bp, provenance.

### Server

`/api/name/<ticker>` returns the series (dates, discount, mu, sd, price,
nav, held_w), the fills, the borrow series, the attributes, and the
recent sessions. Validate the ticker against the frozen universe plus the
27 (a name in the universe csv but not traded gets the charts and "not in
the live universe" in the header). Unknown ticker → 404 with a JSON
reason. Reads only the cached panels and files.

### Acceptance

- `#name/NAD` renders in under 300ms on a warm server.
- The z shown in the header equals the z recomputed from the chart's last
  discount, mu, sd to 2 decimals (assert in a test against
  `_signal_frame`).
- Ex-date ticks appear on the discount chart and none are fabricated for
  names with no distribution history.
- Both themes; the shaded sd bands pass 3:1 against the plane.

### Do not

- Do not add a chart library.
- Do not smooth, resample, or interpolate any series for display; gaps
  are gaps.
