### What to measure, precisely

The sleeve decides at 17:15 ET on day $t$ using the close $C_t$. The order
is MOC and fills in the closing auction of $t+1$. The backtest assumes the
fill is exactly $C_{t+1}$ and the position earns from $t+2$. So there are
two gaps, and only one of them is execution:

- **Delay** $= s_i (C_{t+1} - C_t)/C_t$: the overnight move between decision
  and fill. The backtest **already pays this** (it is why weights are
  shifted two days). Report it, but it is not a cost the desk controls.
- **Execution** $= s_i (\text{VWAP}_{fill} - C_{t+1})/C_{t+1} +
  \text{commission}/\text{notional}$: what we paid relative to the official
  close the model assumed. For a MOC order this *should* be near zero; the
  deviations are penny rounding, partial fills across prints, and, the
  important one, **our own order moving the closing print**.

$s_i = +1$ for buys, $-1$ for sells, so a positive number is a cost.

Breakeven 32.6bp is one-way, per unit of turnover. Compare the
execution term to it, and to the modelled half-spread from `costs.yaml`.

**The impact test MOC hides.** If our MOC order moves the close, the fill
looks perfect against $C_{t+1}$ and the cost appears the next morning as a
reversal. Test: for each name, compare the $t+1 \to t+2$ open-to-close
reversal on sessions we traded it against sessions we did not, signed by
our side. Report the mean and t. This is the only way to see impact when
you benchmark against the close you helped set.
