The desk

What this project is

We operate a systematic credit strategy on a $500,000 Interactive Brokers paper account. The capital is simulated; the prices, orders, fills and trading costs are real. The system runs unattended, refreshing its data each weekday, assessing whether trading is safe, transmitting its orders, recording the executions, and raising an alert on failure.

This year we intend to keep it running, judge it against rules we committed to in writing before launch, and research what comes next.

The trade

The strategy, in plain terms

A closed-end fund trades on the exchange like a stock, with one twist: the number of shares is fixed forever. The fund publishes what everything it owns is worth every day, but the share price can drift far from that value, because nobody can create or redeem shares to pull it back.

That is the whole point. An ordinary fund has banks who swap shares for the underlying bonds and back again, so any gap gets arbitraged away within minutes. We watched that machine kill our two previous strategies: the gap on high-yield funds shrank from 188 basis points in 2008 to 3.8 by 2026. A closed-end fund has no such mechanism, so its gap averages roughly 150× wider.

Our strategy compares each fund's gap to its own history, buys the ones trading unusually cheap, shorts the ones trading unusually rich, and holds equal dollars on each side, so it does not care whether the market rises or falls. Across twenty years of testing, 99.5% of its returns are unexplained by moves in credit, rates, stocks, or volatility.

We are hiring on the real numbers, not the flattering ones.

Trading costs remain the open risk. These funds are small and expensive to trade, and the first live day established that execution cost is a larger constraint than signal quality: our fills came in about nine times worse than modelled, which is fatal if it persists. We identified the cause and shipped a correction, but have not yet been able to test it. After correcting for an entry price the backtest assumed but which is not obtainable, the expected Sharpe ratio is approximately 0.51, not the 0.82 recorded at deployment.

Measuring execution cost and reducing it is the largest single piece of this year's work.

Open roles

Who we are hiring

No finance background is required for any of these. Our documentation teaches the finance from first principles, and assumes you know none of it.

Quant Researcher

2–4 seats

You test new strategy ideas against our gating process. Most ideas die, and finding out why quickly is the actual skill. Your first job on a promising result is to try to kill it, not to report it.

What you need Comfortable Python with pandas. Basic statistics: regressions, t-tests, and a working understanding of what overfitting is. The temperament to watch your favourite idea fail a test and let it go.

Execution & Infrastructure

1–2 seats

You own the pipes: the broker connection, the schedulers, the order flow, and the measurement of what trading actually costs us. This role has taught us more than any backtest, because live systems fail in ways research code never reveals.

What you need Solid Python and real patience for debugging things that fail silently. Any experience with APIs, scheduled jobs, or production systems is a strong plus.

Risk & Reporting

1–2 seats

You watch the live book. Each week you compare what the strategy did against what the backtest said it should do: returns, exposures, drawdowns, and slippage. This is where you learn how a real desk stays honest with itself.

What you need Basic Python, and the ability to write a clear plain-English summary that the whole team will actually read. Precision matters more than speed here.

The year

Goals and timeline

Two dates carry real weight: the 60-session review, where we judge the strategy against rules committed to in writing before launch, and January, when a second strategy trades its only window of the year.

September
Recruit and onboard. Everyone runs the backtest on their own machine and reads the summer report. Get the broker connection reliable again, since the system has not traded since August.
Oct – Nov
Accumulate live sessions to reach the 60-session review. Is live performance and slippage inside what we promised ourselves we would accept? If not, we shut it down, as written.
Nov – Dec
Pre-register the January trade so its rules are frozen before any money moves. Begin research on the next data sources.
January
Run the January trade. It has been positive in 20 of 23 years and moves independently of the main strategy, so the two together are worth more than either alone.
Feb – Mar
Continue research. Decide whether the main book gets resized, based on measured live evidence rather than confidence.
April
Final report: live results against backtest, everything we learned, and a handoff document for next year's team.
