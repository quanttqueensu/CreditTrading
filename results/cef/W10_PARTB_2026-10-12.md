## What Part B was declared to be, and what was run

The signal demeans z across every eligible name. That makes the book a mixture
of two bets:

- **selection within a group**: which PIMCO fund is rich against the other PIMCO
  funds;
- **timing between groups**: multi against hy against loan against muni.

Nobody chose that mixture. The decomposition is exact:
`z_i − z̄ = (z̄_g − z̄) + (z_i − z̄_g)`, that is, between plus within.

| Part B item (as archived) | done? |
|---|---|
| 1. IC by component, clustered by date, by era, fit and holdout | **Yes**, fit period only |
| 2. "The harness table, each book at its natural turnover and matched to 17.6×/yr" | **Not run. It is not zero-trial in substance.** A WITHIN book that is renormalised, vol-targeted and banded on its own is a new portfolio scored against LIVE at matched turnover. That is a variant comparison, and WITHIN-only is the graveyard's "hard group neutrality". In its place is an **exact split of the deployed book's own P&L** (§5), the same kind of split W4 §5 made |
| 3. Correlation and attribution | Yes, on the exact P&L legs. The no-intercept regression the work order asks for is trivial for an exact split: both coefficients are 1 |
| 4. Borrow by book | As borrow by group on the deployed holdings, 2021-09-13 → 2022-12-30 (§7) |
| 5. Random-group negative control, 20 draws | Yes (§3), with a correction to what it should expect |
| 6. Group-by-group within IC | Yes |

**Two definitions of "between" are reported, and neither is chosen on results.**

- *Algebraic*: the identity above for every group.
- *Declared*: the work order's rule that "a group with fewer than 3 eligible names
  is between-only". That rule was written for 17 names. On v7's 13 names it makes
  hy (2 names) and muni (2) between-only too, so only multi keeps a within part.

The note leads with the algebraic split, because its between part is the pure
group-level quantity that a Part D conditioner acts on.

## 2. The signal: slopes and rank ICs

Pooled slope of the demeaned forward return [t+2, t+3] on each component, in bp
per unit z, date-clustered t. The two parts are orthogonal on every date, so the
deployed slope is exactly their variance-weighted average. "var sh b" is the
between part's share of the signal's cross-sectional variance. "cov sh b" is its
share of the predictive covariance.

**Algebraic rule:**

## 3. Negative control: random groups

The labels are shuffled 20 times, keeping the group sizes (8/2/2/1), with seed
20261011.

**A correction to the archived expectation.** The work order expected "a random
grouping's between component [to] have IC near zero". That is wrong. Suppose
forward returns are linear in z with no group structure at all. Then the slope on
either component equals the deployed slope for any grouping, because a random
between part is just a coarse average of z. The test is therefore
`slope(between) − slope(within)`. `test_slopes_equal_for_any_grouping_without_group_structure`
pins the identity on synthetic data.

| | between | within | b − w | t(b − w) | var sh b | cov sh b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| real groups | −3.29 | −9.07 | **+5.78** | 1.18 | 0.403 | 0.196 |
| random: mean of 20 | −8.05 | −6.23 | −1.82 | −0.40 | 0.366 | 0.423 |
| random: min / max | −13.88 / −0.45 | −13.41 / −3.28 | −9.42 / +12.97 | −1.95 / +3.08 | | |

The real difference ranks above **18 of 20** random draws. Grouping does
something in the direction "selection pays more than timing", but at the deployed
set's sample size it is not significant.

## 5. The deployed P&L, split exactly

The held book H is split as B + W:

- **B** spreads each group's **net** held weight equally over the group's held
  names: the between leg.
- **W** is each name's deviation from that, which is group-neutral: the within
  leg.

Both are functions of H on the decision date, so `evaluate()` applies the same
shift(2) to them. The legs add to the deployed P&L to 8.7e−18. B is further split
into the book's own net exposure and the pure group spread.

The table is on total returns, gross only. Costs belong to the traded total and
are not split.

| window | leg | ann % | vol % | gross SR | share of total return |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| live 2013-06 → 2022 | **TOTAL (deployed)** | 6.21 | 6.50 | 0.96 | 1.00 |
| | between (group nets) | 0.68 | 3.57 | 0.19 | 0.11 |
| | of which the book's net | 0.65 | 0.94 | 0.69 | 0.10 |
| | of which the **group spread** | **0.03** | 3.34 | **0.01** | **0.00** |
| | **within (group-neutral)** | **5.53** | 5.38 | **1.03** | **0.89** |
| fit 2005–22 | TOTAL | 3.29 | 4.73 | 0.69 | 1.00 |
| | between | 0.36 | 2.60 | 0.14 | 0.11 |
| | within | 2.93 | 3.92 | 0.75 | 0.89 |

By era the between leg ran at −0.44, +1.29 and +0.76%/yr, and the within leg at
+2.68, +4.42 and +5.73%/yr. Within is positive in every era and is the larger
leg in every era.

**Risk.** On live days the between leg is 30.2% of the total's variance, within
68.5% and 2cov +1.3%. ρ = +0.015 (by era: 2015–19 +0.164, 2020–22 −0.183).

**Gross.** Mean gross held was 1.206. The held-book mix
`Σ|B| / (Σ|B| + Σ|W|)` averaged 0.350 (median 0.341, p90 0.531, max 0.811).
§8 applies the same measure to today's book.

**By group** (live 2013-06 → 2022): between-leg P&L was −0.72%/yr for multi,
+0.57 for hy, +0.07 for muni and +0.75 for loan. Each group's net weight as a
share of gross:

| group | mean | mean \|·\| | p95 \|·\| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| multi | +0.010 | 0.192 | 0.406 |
| hy | +0.051 | 0.197 | 0.413 |
| muni | +0.008 | 0.010 | 0.107 |
| loan | −0.038 | 0.074 | 0.220 |

**Declared rule.** Between is 1.34%/yr (SR 0.36, of which group spread 0.69%/yr,
SR 0.20) and within 4.87%/yr (SR 0.94). The extra between return is the hy-pair
and muni spreads that this rule books as "between".

**Part C's own arithmetic, INDICATIVE ONLY.** Part C splits the budget as
`(v_w, v_b) ∝ (IR_w − ρ IR_b, IR_b − ρ IR_w)`. Fed the gross deployed legs, this
gives the between leg 15% of the weight (algebraic) or 26% (declared), against an
accidental mix of 35% (algebraic, mean). These are not Part C's inputs. Part C
uses separately built, net-of-borrow sleeves at matched turnover.

## 10. What this implies for C2's up-to-5 CEF trials

1. **Settle the evaluation set before any C-trial.** This is a team-lead call.
   - The deployed fit sample is the official baseline (W4). It is frozen on 56% of
     sessions and holds almost no munis.
   - Today's book is two-thirds a group bet against munis and JFR.
   - A C2 test scored only on the deployed fit sample cannot see the bet the book
     is making now. One scored on the WIDE set can, but the WIDE set is "context"
     under the current convention.
   - The same question applies to C1 and C4.
2. **Part C (1 trial): worth it, and first.**
   - Prior: a derived split that tilts toward within. Both sets have within paying
     more per unit; on the WIDE set the between bet is paid, so expect a non-zero
     budget for it.
   - Part C's last row, "between via cheapest-to-borrow", matters: the between bet's
     short side carries 68.5% of the 2021–22 bill.
   - Kill test, as archived: net@15 − borrow is not better than LIVE by the archived
     margin at matched turnover, or BR_eff does not rise, or the 2023+ row
     disagrees in sign.
   - Re-measure every input at run time (H14).
3. **Part D: cut from 4 to at most 2, and run it only after Part C.**
   - **muni**: untestable on the deployed fit set (both names never eligible). It
     needs the WIDE set, or it is D7.
   - **loan**: the work order fits it on the six loan names in the 27, which would
     consume the 27-name set that the 2026-10-09 ruling opened for event trades.
     It **conflicts with E1** (plan order 2): whichever runs first takes the set.
   - **hy**: its within-group selection is zero (52% hit rate), but a group-level
     conditioner acts on between, not within. It is testable.
   - **multi**: testable, but the repo proxy starts 2014-08-22.
   - **If Part C gives the between sleeve zero budget, Part D has nothing left to
     condition and closes at zero trials.**

So C2's realistic cost is **1 trial, plus at most 2 conditional on Part C**, not 5.
