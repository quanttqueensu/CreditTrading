# SPAC redemption at IBKR, and gate G-SPAC re-run on IBKR's terms — 2026-10-19

**What this is.** The team lead ruled on 2026-10-19 that SPAC redemptions go
through IBKR, which is being opened as a second broker for equities only, and
that the SPAC gate is re-run on IBKR's terms. The 2026-10-15 census
(`results/events/SPAC_TRUST_CENSUS_2026-10-15.md`, "the census") stopped at
G-S1. The reason was that Alpaca does not document whether a Trading API
account can redeem at a SPAC vote. This note answers that question for IBKR
from IBKR's public documents. It then re-runs the census gate with IBKR's
listings, commission and fees.

**Zero trials. EVENTS is unchanged.** No trade return is computed. The yields
are the census's terms-implied yields, as of the 2026-10-15 close, less IBKR's
costs. Nothing was logged in to and no broker was connected. Every IBKR fact
comes from a public page or a public file.

**Reproduce.**

```bash
python3 scripts/events/spac_trust_census.py --ibkr            # fetches IBKR's public listing and shortstock file (about 40 requests)
python3 scripts/events/spac_trust_census.py --ibkr --offline  # the same, from the cached 2026-10-19 files
python3 -m pytest scripts/events/tests/test_spac_trust_census.py
```

`--ibkr` reads only the census's committed outputs and IBKR's public files. It
builds no EDGAR client, makes no Alpaca call and reads no credentials. Its
outputs, under `results/events/`:

- `spac_ibkr_gate_live_2026-10-19.csv`: one row per live SPAC (298). Columns:
  the IBKR contract (symbol, conid, primary exchange, IBKR's description), each
  listing check in its own column, median-ADV capacity, and the breakeven
  tickets.
- `spac_ibkr_gate_summary_2026-10-19.json`: every figure in this note.
- ISINs and CUSIPs were read only for a cross-check. They are not written to
  any tracked file, because CUSIPs are licensed and this repository is public.

## 0. The answer

- **IBKR documents SPAC redemption in writing; Alpaca does not.** IBKR's FAQ is
  titled "How do I request a Special Purpose Acquisition Company (SPAC)
  redemption?" It says the request goes "through the Corporate Actions tool"
  in Client Portal [V].
- **The channel is manual, every time.**
  - The election is made in Client Portal: Help, then Support Center, then
    Corporate Actions Manager, then "Choice Corporate Actions" [V]. If an offer
    is missing from the tool, the fallback is a web ticket [V].
  - **There is no API route.** IBKR's public Web API spec has 119 paths and
    none of them is a corporate-action election. The TWS API documentation
    index names none either. The TWS API page says a feature missing from TWS
    "will not be available on the API side either" [V for the documents;
    [U] for any undocumented route].
  - **There are no standing instructions.** "IBKR does not accept default or
    standing instructions from clients with respect to voluntary corporate
    actions" [V].
- **The fee is USD 100 per SPAC redemption request** [V, IBKR FAQ]. IBKR's own
  pages disagree on this:
  - IBKR's fee table lists "All Other (Mandatory, Voluntary, With Options,
    etc) | Free".
  - The same table charges a DWAC at "USD 100 per settled transaction".
  - **This note uses USD 100**, the figure on the SPAC-specific page. It also
    shows **USD 200** in case the DWAC fee is charged on top. Whether it is,
    is [U].
  - Alpaca's fee is the same USD 100 per election (census §2.3). **The two
    brokers do not differ on price. They differ on whether the election is
    documented.**
- **The cut-off is the transfer agent's deadline, and IBKR's falls before it.**
  - The FAQ says agent deadlines "may be earlier than the redemption date
    announced by the company". It also says "Typically, the redemption date is
    two days prior to the meeting date" [V].
  - IBKR's general rule is "12:00 ET the day prior to the expiration of the
    offer" [V text]. The page states it for "All Other Regions"; that this
    covers US SPACs is [S].
  - The cash arrives "within 1-3 weeks" [V].
- **IBKR paper accounts: unknown [U], and probably no.** IBKR calls paper "a
  simulator with no execution or clearing abilities". Its FAQ lists "Limited
  access to Client Portal functions" among paper's limits [V]. A redemption
  is a DWAC of real shares to the transfer agent. Nothing IBKR publishes says
  paper can do one.
- **Tradable at IBKR: 287 of the 298 live SPACs.**
  - IBKR lists 288 of them. One listed name, MYX, fails the name check and is
    left out rather than forced in.
  - The 287 are exactly the Alpaca-tradable commons less MYX. **No live SPAC is
    at IBKR but missing at Alpaca.**
  - For the gate: 280 IBKR-tradable names are in the redeemable pool, and 273
    of them have a deadline ahead.
- **Yield after IBKR's costs** (§3; median spread over the bill [model]).
  - **The liquidation route has no fee.** The spread is +1.99% gross and
    +1.61% at 30bp for the pool. Only IBKR's commission comes off, and it
    amortises to about 5bp a year.
  - **The vote route is ticket-dependent.** It pays USD 100 at the exit:
    - pool: **−0.36%** at USD 5k, **+0.79% / +0.43%** (gross / 30bp) at USD 10k,
      **+1.50% / +1.17%** at USD 25k;
    - near the deadline and below the accrued trust: **−0.24%** at USD 10k,
      **+1.29% / +0.64%** at USD 25k, **+1.89% / +1.07%** at USD 50k.
  - **The no-further-interest yield near the deadline is negative even on the
    fee-free liquidation route** (−0.67% gross of the grid), as the census found.
- **Capacity that repays the fee:** **21 of 47** near-deadline names below the
  accrued trust. Together they hold **USD 3.26M** of median-ADV capacity, for a
  one-off **USD 28k over bills gross, USD 18k at 30bp** [model].
  - Three names carry 53% of that capacity.
  - Two of the 21 (TVA, BEAG) were 9–10 days from their deadline on
    2026-10-15, so they are already gone.
- **Workload:** about **53–64 manual elections a year** if all 21 slots are held.
  At USD 100 each, that is about USD 5–6k of fees.
- **Gate verdict: NOT PASSED. It no longer fails because IBKR is silent; it
  fails on paper and on four open terms.**
  - **G-S1(a)** passes on IBKR's documentation, for a live account.
  - **G-S1(b)** is documented in general terms only.
  - **G-S1(c)**, what paper does, is not shown.
  - **G-S2** passes on the model only at tickets of USD 10k or more (pool) and
    USD 25k or more (near the deadline).
  - **G-S3** is the team lead's sizing call.
  - **G-S4** is unchanged and passes.
  - **A new blocker:** every election is a manual Client Portal action, while
    the book's orders run on a schedule. The question for IBKR support is in §6.

## 2. Which live SPACs are tradable at IBKR

**Method** (`ibkr_listing`, `ibkr_match`).

- **The listing.** IBKR's public "Products and Exchange Search" page calls an
  endpoint that lists every STK contract on an exchange. The census found
  every live tradable common on Nasdaq, NYSE or NYSE American, so those three
  lists were read: 4,018, 7,144 and 7,143 rows. The two NYSE lists include
  contracts that trade there but are listed elsewhere. `isPrimeExchId` marks
  the primary listing.
- **The candidate symbols.** The census's Alpaca common symbol comes first.
  The EDGAR tickers follow, with unit, warrant and right suffixes removed.
- **Checks.** Each check is its own CSV column, and none is folded into
  another:
  1. **Name.** `name_score` (the census's CRSP matcher) must reach at least
     0.75 against IBKR's description, with the class tail removed. Failing
     that, IBKR's description must be a truncation of the census name of at
     least 12 letters (`ibkr_truncated_prefix`).
  2. **Shortstock conid.** IBKR's public shortstock file must carry the
     symbol with the same contract id. This is a second IBKR file, not an
     independent source.
  3. **ISIN against EDGAR.** The ISIN's country prefix must match EDGAR's
     state of incorporation (E9 → KY, D8 → VG, a US state → US). This check
     is reported only.
- **What "tradable" means here.** `tradable_ibkr` = listed, one conid, and the
  name check passed. **It is a listing, not a promise that our account may
  trade the name.** IBKR's trading permissions are per account and not public.

**Disclosed change.** On the first run the name check used `name_score`
alone, and 16 of the 288 listed commons failed it. All 16 are IBKR truncating
long names at a fixed width before adding the class:

- "OAKTREE ACQUISITION CORP I-A" is Oaktree Acquisition Corp. III Life Sciences;
- "CHENGHE ACQUISITION II-CL A" is Chenghe Acquisition III Co.

Cutting through "III" leaves "I" or "II", which the matcher reads as a
different number. The truncation rule was written **after** these rows were
seen. It admits 15 of them. **MYX** ("MAYWOOD ACQUISITION C2-CL-A", Maywood
Acquisition Corp. 2) fails both rules and is left out rather than forced in.
`name_rule` in the CSV shows which rule admitted each name. All 15 truncations
pass the conid check, 14 pass the ISIN check, and ATII has no EDGAR state code.

## 3. Yield after IBKR's costs

**The arithmetic** (`ibkr_net_terms`; the hand recomputation of one name
agrees to machine precision):

- **The position.** A position buys floor(ticket ÷ price) whole shares. It pays
  the Fixed commission and the CAT fee, the desk's grid of 5 / 15 / 30bp on
  the buy, and the exit fee of the route.
- **No sale.** The exit is the redemption, so there is no sale.
- **No fee.** With no fee and no lag, this reduces exactly to the census grid,
  which a test checks.
- **The net yield.**
  - M = 1 + gross × days / 365;
  - net = (M − costs ÷ value − 1) × 365 / (days + lag);
  - spread = net − bill.
- **The lag.** IBKR pays the cash "within 1-3 weeks". The 21-day case is that
  bound, with the cash earning nothing meanwhile.
- **Inputs.** The census's `yield_total_next` [model] and `yield_next` (no
  further interest), to the next deadline. The bill is 4.10% (DTB3,
  2026-10-14).
- **The routes.** A "liquidation" exit needs no election, so it pays no fee. A
  "vote" exit is an election at a deal or extension vote, so it pays USD 100.
  The USD 200 case is the same election with the DWAC fee charged on top.

The gross headline, with the cost grid beside it, is in each cell: **gross / 5bp
/ 15bp / 30bp**. Medians are across names.

**Caveats on these numbers.**

- **The capacity is concentrated.** Of the 21 near-deadline names, CUB, TVA
  and DBCA hold 53% of the USD 3.26M.
- **Two of the 21 are already gone.** TVA and BEAG were 9 and 10 days from
  their next deadline on 2026-10-15. By §1's purchase rule they could not be
  bought and elected after this note's date.
- **The near-deadline gain is per cycle.** That cycle is the median 107 days to
  the next deadline.
- **The whole-pool figure is one-off.** It is spread over a median of about
  311 days.

**Workload** (`ibkr_election_load`, from the census's own counts for 2024–25).

- **How often the first event needs an election.** In 2024–25 there were 247
  extension votes, 92 completed deals and 41 liquidations. So **89%** of first

- **For the book.** **Holding all 21 names means about 53–64 elections a

- **IBKR does not track the events for us.** IBKR notifies "on a bests [sic] effort
  basis" and lists an offer only "a few days after the initial announcement",
  so the desk must track vote dates itself. The census already reads them
