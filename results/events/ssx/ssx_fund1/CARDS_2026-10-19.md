## 1. Card A1 — fund share issuance into premiums

**Mechanism.** A CEF or BDC trading above NAV sells new common shares: an at-the-market (ATM)
programme off a shelf, or an underwritten follow-on. A registered CEF may not sell common below
current NAV except by rights offering, majority consent, conversion or SEC order
(15 U.S.C. 80a-23(b) [V]), so its issuance happens only at a premium, and the extra supply
caps it. A BDC may sell below NAV after an annual shareholder authorisation
(15 U.S.C. 80a-62, ICA §63(2): majority of holders and of non-affiliated holders, at the last
annual meeting within one year [V]).

**Named loser.** Buyers at the premium: retail yield buyers on the exchange, and allottees of
follow-ons, diluted toward NAV as issuance continues. **Why it persists:** the buyer prices on
yield, not NAV; ATM sales are drip-fed and are disclosed only later (next supplement, N-CSR,
10-Q); the sponsor earns fees on new assets; and about half the issuers are not shortable at
Alpaca (below). Akhigbe & Madura (2001) report that CEFs issue when the premium is high and
liquid, with a significant negative valuation effect at the offering and weak returns over
three years [V abstract, RePEc]. Their sample years were not read [U].

**Event clock and first point-in-time source.** The supplement's EDGAR filing.
- **Before 2020-08-01** both registered CEFs and BDCs filed supplements under Rule 497.
- **From 2020-08-01** they file under Rule 424(b)(2)/(3)/(5). The 2020 offering reform made
  Rule 424 "the exclusive rule" (Release 33-10771, effective 2020-08-01; K&L Gates [V],
  SEC compliance guide [V]).
- **Measured switch:** listed BDCs filed 0 supplements as 424B in 2010–19 and 1,700 as 497.
  Both types switch to 424B in 2020–21 (`a1_listed_supplements_by_year_family.csv`). This
  corrects my own first assumption that BDCs always used 424; the docstring records it.
- The index carries the filing date only. A filing is public by 17:30 ET on that date. The
  exact acceptance time needs the submissions API (not fetched for the population). Rule: an
  acceptance after 16:00 ET is decided next session.
- An ATM is a **state**, not a point event: active from its supplement until it is exhausted
  or terminated. The sales themselves are seen only with a lag (CRSP shares, N-CSR).

**Expression at Alpaca.**
- **Standalone:** short the issuing fund (whole shares, MOC), hedged with the sector ETF:
  HYG, BKLN, AGG, PFF or EMB, all shortable [doc: CEF_CORP_ACTION_CENSUS §5].
- **Munis have no shortable ETF** (MUB, VTEB, TFI, HYD, SUB all hard to borrow
  [doc, 2026-10-18]). IEF hedges duration only.
- **Preferred: as a conditioner on the live book** (no new long, or a short tilt, while a
  fund runs an active ATM at a premium). That keeps it on the shortable names the book
  already trades.
- **IBKR** if borrow is the blocker.

Notes on the sample:
- **Design.** 140 fund-years were drawn, seeded and stratified by type × era, and 339
  documents were read (at most 4 per fund-year).
- **Classifier check.** The rules were checked by hand on a seeded 20-document subset: 19 of
  20 were right. The miss changed the rights rule, and all 9 remaining rights labels were
  then re-read and confirmed.
- **Why the per-year figures are lower bounds.** 30 fund-years had more than 4 supplements,
  and only 4 of each were read.
- **CRSP cross-check.** The share-growth count includes DRIP issuance, which is also new
  supply sold above NAV. It agrees in magnitude with the sample estimate.
- **Proxy hits.** 24 of the 26 below-NAV proxy hits were read by hand and are genuine. One is
  false (NMFC 2014, a BDC-election withdrawal) and one is ambiguous (BKCC 2020).

**Premium at filing** (state variable; pre-2023, outside the 44): 50 of 65 common-issue
supplements (27 funds) state a last sale price above the first NAV per share they state.
That is 77%, with a median of +9.0%. **Indicative only [U]:** the NAV matched is often a
period-end table figure, and a BDC's NAV is a quarter-end figure.

**Capacity.** Pre-event ADV over 20 sessions ending 2 before the filing, ≤ 2022, ex-44, using
each fund-year's first filing:
- BDC: median **$2.2m**; 75% ≥ $1m; 17% ≥ $10m (n 310).
- Registered CEF: median **$1.2m**; 56% ≥ $1m; 1.3% ≥ $10m (n 749).

Under the corp catalogue's rule (5% of book at ≤ 5% of one session's ADV), most events
carry a $1m book.

**Borrow** (Alpaca, 2026-10-19 15:58 ET; every shortable name was also ETB):
- All 2024–25 listed-supplement filers: **113 of 199** shortable (192 found). BDC 29 of 48;
  registered CEF 83 of 149.
- Sampled 2024–25 ATM issuers: **12 of 23**. ARCC, GBDC, BXSL, CSWC, HRZN, SAR, NMZ, NAC,
  NMI, NHS and RSF are not shortable.
- A ticker bug was found and fixed before this read. The alphabetical first SEC ticker was a
  baby bond for 36 of 199 funds (PFLA for PFLT, SAJ for SAR).

**Graveyard neighbours.**
- **`single-fund-PD`:** a "premium" against a stale NAV is NAV staleness, not dislocation.
  BDC NAVs are quarter-end by construction.
- **The core z-score:** it already ranks rich funds on its short side. A1 must add
  information beyond the z (the supply event), or it is the same signal.
- **E1-band:** premium reversion.
- **Not M7:** distribution data plays no part.
- **How A1 differs:** it is a dated, filed supply decision by the sponsor, not a level.

**D4 prior.** Moderate risk.
- An ETF-hedged short leaves the CEF discount factor, which the core book also carries.
- Premium funds skew to equity, option-income and MLP CEFs. The population is not tagged
  by asset class, so the credit share is [U]. A credit-only restriction shrinks E.

**Counter.** As a short-side conditioner on the live book: **CEF**. As a standalone event
sleeve: **EVENTS**.

**Kill gate.** < 30 independent fund-events/yr, or < half shortable today.
- **Events: PASS.** Point estimates are 55–67 common-issuing fund-years/yr; CRSP shows 45–94.
  The 2020–22 Wilson lower bound (29.2) touches the line.
- **Borrow: PASS, marginally.** 12 of 23 ATM issuers (52%, Wilson 33–71%) and 113 of 199
  filers (57%). The kill number sits inside the interval.
- Re-read the flags on several dates before relying on this (landmine 5).

**Score E·S·F = 3·2·1 = 6.**
- E 3: more than 50 a year, continuous.
- S 2: documented negative valuation effect, old sample, not shown to have vanished.
- F 1: the short leg is hard to borrow for half the names, and munis have no hedge.

## 2. Card A3 — BDC buyback plans that buy only below NAV (10b5-1)

**Mechanism.** The BDC's agent must buy whenever the price is below the most recently
reported NAV per share, increasing volume as the price falls, until a size cap or an
expiry. A mechanical bid exists while the price is below NAV, and it disappears at
exhaustion or expiry. **Named loser:** holders who rely on the floor past expiry, and
short-sellers squeezed while it is active. **Why it persists:** the plans sit mostly around
listings, when supply from newly liquid holders is largest.

**Terms are disclosed point-in-time [V, read in the filings]:**
- **ORCC**, IPO prospectus 0001193125-19-196326 (2019-07-18): $150m; buys "when the market
  price per share is below the most recently reported net asset value per share". Commences
  30 days after closing. Ends at the earliest of 18 months (tolled during suspension), $150m,
  or other events.
- **NCDL**, 424B1 0001628280-24-002305: $100m, from 60 days after the Reg M restricted
  period, for 12 months.
- **MSDL**, 424B1 0001104659-24-006251: $100m.
- **Caveat:** the "tolled" clause makes the exact expiry date not strictly point-in-time.

**Event clock.** The IPO prospectus (424B1, or 497 before 2020-08) dates the plan. Expiry is
the closing date plus the stated months. Seasoned plans appear in 10-Q/10-K repurchase notes.

**Expression.** Short the BDC around expiry with an HYG or BKLN hedge; BDCs are about half
shortable today (above).

**MEASURED counts.**
- **BDC listings:** **3.0 a year**, 2010–24 (CRSP first name record of BDC-mapped permnos:
  5, 7, 5, 4, 7, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 4, 1, 0, 5).
- **IPO prospectuses:** of 13 BDC IPO prospectuses in cache, 9 carry a below-NAV 10b5-1
  plan: TSLX 2014, TCG 2017, BCSF 2018, ORCC 2019, RWAY 2021, BXSL 2021, NCDL, MSDL and
  PSBD 2024.
- **Seasoned BDCs** (seeded sample of 48 listed-BDC 10-Qs, 33 BDCs): **0 of 12** (2010–15),
  **1 of 12** (2016–19), **1 of 12** (2020–22), **4 of 12** (2023–25).
  - Plans seen: ORCC (IPO), CSWC ($10m 2016 and $20m 2021 programmes), MSC Income (listing,
    $65m), BXSL (IPO), and FSK's affiliated-purchaser plan.
  - Size was stated in 5 of 6 plans, expiry in 4 of 6.
  - Listed BDCs file 10-Qs at 25–58 BDCs a year (`a3_bdc_10q_summary.json`).
  - Implied BDCs with an active plan: about **4 a year in 2016–22** (Wilson ceiling about
    17), and about 17 in 2023–25 (counts only).

**Capacity:** BDC median ADV $2.2m (A1 table). **Borrow:** BDCs 29 of 48 shortable today.

**Graveyard neighbours.** `S3-forced-flow` (a mechanical flow that ends) and conditional
tenders N1 (`CONDITIONAL_TENDER_CENSUS_2026-10-13.md`). The CEF corp census ranked $/Dutch
BDC tenders "too few to rank".

**D4 prior.** High: a small-BDC short is credit beta. **Counter:** EVENTS.

**Kill gate:** < 30 plan expiries a year, or terms not disclosed point-in-time.
- Terms: disclosed.
- Count: **KILLED.** Expiries cannot exceed the number of plan holders. That is about 3 a
  year from listings, plus about 4 seasoned plans a year in 2010–22, far below 30.

**Score 0·1·2 = 0.**

## 3. Card A7 — fund leverage changes

**Mechanism.** A fund redeems or refinances preferred shares (a Rule 23c-2 notice, form
N-23C-2), issues notes or preferred, or has its senior securities re-rated. Deleveraging
lowers the common's income and risk before a yield-bought holder base re-prices.

**Named loser:** [U]. If it is common holders through a lower distribution, that is the M7
channel. If it is preferred holders above par at a call, that belongs to CR15/L1 (credit
catalogue). A forced deleveraging is the pod's N6 asset-coverage stress (risk only).

**Event clock.** N-23C-2 filing date (index). The Rule 23c-2 notice period was not
verified [U]. Rating-action date (17g-7, published with a lag: backfilled).

**Identification.**
- N-23C-2 by listed funds at the filing date.
- 17g-7 issuers matched by name to fund-set CIKs with the merger matcher (score ≥ 0.85,
  margin ≥ 0.02, blocked on the sponsor word): **302 issuer names → 298 CIKs** (259
  registered CEFs, 38 BDCs; minimum score 0.86).
- `HS` is the history-start snapshot (138,212 of its 138,295 rows are dated 2012) and is
  excluded.
- **One event is one issuer-day.** Prospect's InterNotes put 722 instrument rows on one
  issuer.

**MEASURED counts.**

165 leverage events on 24 funds; control = the same fund's share of days within ±w of one

| ±30d | 18.8% | 18.2% | 14.7% | 18.3% | 26.8% | 18.1% |
| **±90d (primary)** | **35.8%** | **42.8%** | 31.2% | 43.7% | 44.6% | 41.0% |
| ±180d | 52.1% | 64.6% | 48.6% | 66.0% | 58.9% | 61.9% |

Leverage events are **not** distribution cuts in another coat: overlap is below 50% and
below the control. Ratings run slightly above the control at ±30d (n 56), the one place
worth watching.

**Expression.** Most of these 43-fund events are Nuveen munis: NVG, NEA, NZF, NAD and NAN
together carry 77 of the 165. The four Nuveen book names were not shortable at Alpaca when
spec v7 was set [doc: RESEARCH_STATE trial 49, 2026-09-28; not re-read today], and munis have no shortable ETF. Feasible only at IBKR, or as a risk flag.

**D4 prior:** high (credit/rate beta of a levered fund). **Counter:** EVENTS. Leverage
levels would need N-PORT (NPORT counter).

**Kill gate:** overlap > 50%, or < 30 events a year.
- Overlap: **PASS**.
- Events: **PASS** on N-23C-2 alone by the mean (44 funds a year). Fragile by the minimum
  (19) and the median (35). Ratings alone fail.
- Verdict: **PARK.** Both gates pass, but no source documents an edge on the common (S 1 [U]).

**Score 2·1·1 = 2.**
