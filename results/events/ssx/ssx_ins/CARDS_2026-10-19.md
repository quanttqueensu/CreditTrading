## D1 — clustered open-market insider buying (US operating companies)

**Mechanism and loser.** Directors and officers buy with private information about their firm. Prices barely react, either when they trade or when the trade is reported. In Lakonishok–Lee (2001, 1975–95) "insiders in aggregate are contrarian"; the information is in purchases, not sales, and "driven by ... smaller firms" [V].

- **Loser:** whoever sells after the Form 4 is public, into a price that has not yet absorbed it. In practice that is momentum and attention-driven sellers in small caps.
- **Why it persists:** limits to arbitrage in small names, and low attention (Alldredge–Blank: clustering is stronger "during periods of low investor attention, high uncertainty" [V]).

**Event clock and PIT.** The event is the Form 4 FILING_DATE of the filing that completes a cluster.
- Reg S-T 232.13(a)(4): Forms 3/4/5 transmitted by **10 p.m. ET** are filed that day [V]. A filing dated *t* can therefore arrive after the book's 22:00 ET decision.
- **So: decide at t+1, fill MOC at t+2.**
- The data sets carry no acceptance time.
- Measured: the median trigger lag from the last trade to the event filing is 1 business day (p90 2). Across all code-P purchases, 88.4% are filed within 2 business days of the trade, rising from 87.9% (2006–09) to 90.4% (2020–22) [V].

**Rule (fixed before any outcome; `ssx_ins_insider_census.py` docstring).**
- Purchases: original Form 4 only, code P, acquired, shares > 0, price > 0, common-equity title.
- Insiders: directors and officers only. Ten-percent-only holders (activists) are out.
- A cluster fires when ≥ 3 distinct owners across ≥ 3 distinct filings have trades in the trailing 30 days.
- Cooldown: 90 days per issuer.
- "Clean" excludes single-price, single-date clusters, the signature of an offering or placement: 3,311 of 17,732.

For **2010–22**:
- 10,629 clusters (8,476 clean).
- CRSP match by the symbol as filed: **86.5%** (9,190). By stage: 14,640 exact, 30 by class suffix, 12 ambiguous, 2,485 none, over all years to 2024.
- 6,750 are clean and CRSP common (shrcd 10/11).
- ADV ≥ $0.1m: 5,552. **ADV ≥ $1m: 3,485.** ADV ≥ $10m: 1,579.
- All 12 months carry events in every year.

**Capacity.** The median pre-event ADV is $0.43m–$2.65m by year (2010–22). At the catalogue's rule (usable at book B when ADV ≥ B), $100k is not binding (about 427 a year); at $1m, about 268 a year.

**Expression and borrow.** Long the issuer at MOC; short IWM or the sector SPDR.
- Today IWM, SPY, IJR, XLF, XLK, XLV, XLE, XLI and XBI are shortable and ETB; **KRE is not** [V 2026-10-19].
- No borrow is needed on the long.

**Graveyard neighbour.** "Pre-positioning on activist attacks" was rejected in `results/cef/SCALING_RESEARCH_2026-10-08.md`: a 13D-sized stake is never ours, and targets give the narrowing back by t+2. D1 differs in three ways:
- it **excludes** ten-percent-only filers;
- it follows management's own cash, not an outsider's campaign;
- its literature effect is drift over weeks, not a two-day pop.

D1 is not a stale-price artefact: the input is a dated filing, not price minus a mark.

**D4 prior: HIGH.** Purchases are contrarian and concentrated in small firms [V, LL 2001]. Clusters bunch after sell-offs: March 2020 had **364** clean events, August 2011 **259**, against a monthly median of 47 (2010–22) [V, this census]. So the strategy will load on size, value, short-term reversal and post-crash market beta. An IWM hedge removes beta only. A test must clear alpha t ≥ 3 against FF5 + UMD + short-term reversal.

**Literature.**
- Alldredge–Blank (2019, *JFR* 42(2)): "clustered insider purchases are followed by abnormal returns in excess of 2% during the subsequent month" [V].
- Cohen–Malloy–Pomorski (2012, *JF* 67(3)): opportunistic insiders earn 82bp a month value-weighted; routine insiders about zero [S].
- Jeng–Metrick–Zeckhauser (2003): purchases earn about 37–47bp a month, 1975–96 [S].
- **Post-publication decay for US insider purchases: not found [U].** McLean–Pontiff (2016) has no abstract on IDEAS, so no figure is quoted.

**Counter.** The data sets are a **new source**: a ruling is needed on a new INSIDER counter versus EVENTS.

**Gate.** < 250 → kill. Measured 3,485 → **PASS.**

**E·S·F = 3·2·2 = 12.** F is 2 because live use needs a daily Form 4 XML reader. The quarterly sets lag up to a quarter. That reader is a build, not a request budget this seat had.

## A2 — insider buying in CEFs and BDCs

**Mechanism and loser.** Directors, officers, adviser personnel and the adviser buy the fund's shares. If they buy below NAV, that says the marks are honest and the discount is too wide.
- **Loser:** holders who sell into the discount, typically retail in cash accounts and December tax-loss sellers.
- **Why it persists:** the wrapper has no AP arbitrage.
- **Speculative [U]:** these insiders may buy *because* the discount is wide. Then A2 is the live z-score with a filing date attached. A Missouri dissertation reads CEF insider trading as discount-reversion trading, "not ... superior information" [S; author and year U, repository 403].

**Who files.** 1940 Act §30(h) brings directors, officers, 10% holders, the adviser and the adviser's affiliated persons under Section 16 [S].

**Fund identification.** The repo's fund-CIK rule (`fetch_edgar_events.fund_set`, offline indexes 2008Q1–2025Q4; 2,299 CIKs), **not** SIC 6726, since the index carries no SIC.
- BDC: filed N-54A/N-54C, or files 10-K without N-CSR.
- CEF: files N-CSR.

**Owner classes.** The rule was revised after reading the first pass's owner names, before any outcome existed. That pass's "adviser" class was mostly activists: Saba, Bulldog, the Horejsi trusts.
- Equity-title purchases 2006–25: DO 40,173; **ten-percent-only 11,264 (excluded: activists)**; adviser 6,425; other_person 4,846 (largely portfolio managers and trustees); other_entity 618.

**Measured counts [V]**, as fund-events: the first qualifying purchase after a 90-day gap.

More measurements:
- **CRSP match, 2010–22:** 88.1% (shrcd 14: 3,614, 44: 2,200, 11: 189 over all years).
- **Distinct triggering-owner-month groups:** 150–254 a year for CEFs, against 216–348 events.
- **Purchase size (qualifying):**
  - CEF: median $10.0k, p90 $101k.
  - BDC: median $5.0k, p90 $150k, **p10 $81**, which looks like dividend reinvestment reported as P. Hence the $10k floor.
  - Dollar totals are not reported: fat-fingered prices dominate them.
- **Adviser involved:** 222 of the 2010–22 events (CEF 198, BDC 24), about 17 a year.
- **Holdout, COUNT ONLY:** 347 fund-events on 40 `cef_universe.csv` symbols, 2006–25. Nothing else was computed on them.

**Capacity.** The median listed pre-event ADV is $0.7m–$1.4m; ADV ≥ $10m is only 15 a year.

**Expression and borrow.** Long the fund at MOC; hedge with CEFS or YYY, both shortable and ETB on 2026-10-15 [V, catalogue]. **MUB is not shortable**, so a muni fund has no clean hedge. BDC hedge: not measured [U].

**Graveyard neighbour.** The same activist row as D1. The ten-percent-only exclusion is what keeps A2 out of it.

**D4 prior.** Overlap with CEF-DISC itself, plus credit, muni and BDC credit beta. The first test must be incremental to the live z-score.

**Counter.** A new-source ruling, as for D1. Any return on a universe name **consumes the 27-name holdout**.

**Gate.** < 50 a year → kill. Measured ≥ 86 even on the strictest variant → **PASS.**

**E·S·F = 3·1·2 = 6.** S is 1: no peer-reviewed CEF evidence was found, and the one source found says "not information".

## D5 — IPO quiet-period expiry

**Mechanism and loser.** Underwriter analysts initiate, nearly always favourably, when the quiet period ends. Bradley–Jordan–Ritter (2003, *JF* 58(1), 1996–2000): a five-day abnormal return of 4.1% with coverage against 0.1% without [V]. The run-up comes before the date: the (−2, −1) CMAR is 2.32% against 0.80% on days 0 to +2 [V, BJRW 2004 summary].
- **Loser:** late buyers who absorb the selling that knowledgeable holders postponed [V, BJRW's reading].

**Rule history [V].**
- 25 calendar days.
- Extended to 40 on **9 Jul 2002** (BJRW).
- FINRA 2241(b)(2)(I): "a minimum of 10 days following the date of an initial public offering" for participating underwriters, effective 2015 (adopted SR-FINRA-2014-047; effective dates 25 Sep and 24 Dec 2015).
- **The subparagraph "shall not apply"** after an IPO of an Emerging Growth Company.
- So for most post-2012 IPOs the date is market convention, not rule. A day-25 convention: **[U], not sourced.**

**Decay.** Bradley, Jordan, Ritter and Wolf (2004, *JOIM*), 94 IPOs from January 2001 to mid-July 2002: "we do not find positive abnormal returns ... this anomaly may have disappeared", possibly a bear-market effect [V]. Post-2015 evidence: none found. A 2018 Erasmus thesis calls July 2012–June 2015 "ambiguous" [S].

**Census [V]**, from `ssx_ins_ipo_census.py`; the CRSP rule is stated in its docstring.

## K5 — IPO lock-up expiry (G-K5, run)

- SPACs are excluded by the SPAC census CIKs and by name.
- Ticker from the SEC `company_tickers_exchange.json` (2026-10-12).
