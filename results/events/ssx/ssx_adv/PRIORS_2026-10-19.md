# ssx_adv: adversarial priors for the special-situations expansion (2026-10-19)

**Seat:** the adversary (team lead's request of 2026-10-19). For each candidate
in `results/events/SS_DESK_EXPANSION_2026-10-19.md`, this note gives the most
likely way it dies before it spends a trial.

**ZERO TRIALS.**
- No return of any candidate was computed on our data.
- No `data/` panel and no holdout was read. No counter moves.
- The ledger on this branch reads CEF 52 (bar 2.811) and EVENTS 0
  (`docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md`). The seat prompt's "CEF at 48" is stale.

**Reproduce.** The only computation is on public data of published anomalies:

    python3 scripts/events/ssx/ssx_adv_cz_priors.py --cache /abs/dir --fetch --all

- Output: `cz_priors_2026-10-19.csv` and `cz_priors_stdout_2026-10-19.txt`,
  both beside this note.
- Inputs:
  - Chen–Zimmermann Open Source Asset Pricing, October 2025 release (v2.0.0),
    long-short returns to 2024-12;
  - Ken French's FF5, momentum and short-term reversal factors (files built
    from the 202608 CRSP), to 2026-08;
  - the Pástor–Stambaugh traded liquidity factor, to 2024-12.

**Labels.**
- [V]: fetched and read on 2026-10-19.
- [V calc]: computed by the script above from those public files.
- [S]: a search summary or other secondary source.
- [U]: unverified.
- [doc]: a figure from a repo note, not re-run here.

Survival odds in §3 are **judgement**, not measurement.

## 0. The answer

1. **The published-anomaly base rate is brutal under the desk's D4.**
   - **The test:** each of the 207 Chen–Zimmermann predictors with at least
     120 months over 2005–2024, built as the paper built it, regressed on
     FF5 + UMD + ST_Rev + PS LIQ with Newey-West t.
   - **Both legs (alpha t ≥ 3.0 and every |β| ≤ 0.10): 2 of 207 (1.0%) pass**,
     DivSeason and DownRecomm.
   - **The beta leg alone:** 8 (4%).
   - **The alpha-t leg alone:** 36 (17%). That is the ceiling even with perfect
     factor hedging.
   - **Decay:** the median post-publication mean is **0.40×** the in-sample
     mean, and **31%** are still significant after publication [V calc]. This
     matches McLean–Pontiff's 58% decay [doc §12b] and Hou–Xue–Zhang's 65% fail
     at |t| 1.96 with value weighting and NYSE breakpoints [V].
   - **Consequence:** every mainstream-equity row (D1–D9) starts below one in
     six, before costs and borrow.
2. **Our execution convention kills the announcement-day rows.** Under
   `shift(2)`, an after-hours event is filled at the next session's close.
   - **D8:** the S&P 400/600 move is entirely on the first day after the
     announcement, then flat (IHS Markit [V]).
   - **D6:** 64% of Item 4.02 filings land 16:00–20:00, so the −1.10% day-1
     CAR happens before our fill [V vendor].
   - **G1:** Rule 204 close-outs are due by the *open* [V].
   - **E1:** the CEF merger gap closes at announcement [doc].
3. **The stale-price well reappears in nine rows:** C1, C6, C7, D2, A3, A7,
   A16, the B flags, and SPAC daily closes.
   - C7 is `leadlag` with a new label.
   - D2 is `single-fund-PD` on BDCs.
4. **The rows most likely to survive D1 and D4 are contractual-convergence
   trades hedged inside one asset**: SPACs below trust, the dual-class spread,
   NAV tenders, the spin-off removal close, and stock-for-stock merger arb.
   - If they die, they die of D2, D5 or D7, not D1 or D4.
   - Several earn too little to be worth Reg T gross that the core book could
     use.

## 1. The table

The columns: **death** is the most likely D-code and why. **Grave** is the
closest graveyard entry, and whether this is the same mechanism. **D4** names
the factors and the exact test. **Control** is the negative control to declare
before the run (H12). **Prior** is the literature, marked mech[anical] or
beh[avioural]. **Stale?** is the stale-price trap check. **V** is the verdict.

**The standard D4 regression (call it R4)**, used in every equity row unless a
row says otherwise:
- regress daily or monthly P&L on FF5 + UMD + ST_Rev + PS LIQ with HAC t;
- add HYG and IEF for credit rows, and MUB for muni rows;
- **plus a piecewise market β with kinks at −4% and +4% a month**: down for
  put-like books, up for distress shorts that lose in junk rallies.

A linear hedge removes neither kink. Since 2010-01, **23 of 200 months** had
Mkt-RF < −4% [V calc]. Any kink estimate is therefore weak, and its power must
be stated in the pre-registration.

## 2. Five patterns, and the one test for each

1. **A published anomaly with a new label.** D1, D4–D7, A3 and K6 all have a
   Chen–Zimmermann sibling. Every sibling fails the desk's D4 as published.
   - The siblings mostly fail on RMW, SMB or ST_Rev loadings above 0.10, not on
     the market.
   - **One test:** pre-register the R4 regression *and* the hedge instruments
     that will hold each |β| ≤ 0.10 at Alpaca. If no instrument hedges RMW or
     ST_Rev, the row cannot pass and should not run.
2. **The announcement-day trap.** D6, D8, A5, A9, E1 and G1 have their move
   before our fill.
   - **One test (zero trials):** take the filing timestamp from EDGAR's
     acceptance time (`results/events/edgar_acceptance_offsets`). Then measure
     the share of the literature's effect that falls after the
     t+1 close.
3. **The stale-mark well.** C7, D2, A3, A16 and the B flags measure against a
   NAV, an OTC print or a thin close.
   - **One test:** `leadlag`'s, rerun as executable close-to-close with
     `shift(2)`, auction prints, and no-trade days dropped, with a
     no-mechanism control.
4. **The short put.** MA, and G3 if it holds merger arb. **One test:** the
   piecewise β and the PUT-index regression, with power stated for about 23
   crash months.
5. **The distress short.** D4, D6, C2, and part of D1. These are S3-wrapper's
   quality premium with an event label, and they lose in junk rallies.
   **One test:** the up-kink β and an RMW/distress loading, and borrow
   measured at IBKR, not modelled at zero.

**The B flags are not ranked.** As subtract-only rules they cannot fail D4.

- They are cheap at zero trials.
- B4 has the lowest prior, because NAV already contains the mark.

**The bar.** EVENTS reads 0. A declared slate of N is judged at √(2 ln N):
- N = 5: 1.79;
- N = 10: 2.15;
- all 38 rows: 2.70.

For nearly every row, D4's alpha t ≥ 3.0 binds first. **Declare a small slate;
do not run the long tail.**

## 4. The five most likely wastes of a trial

They would look strong on a naive run and die after the trial is spent:

1. **C7**: `leadlag` exactly.
2. **D8**: the effect is on a day we cannot trade.
3. **D2**: `single-fund-PD` on BDCs, plus PEAD's death.
4. **C6**: ex-date arithmetic and borrow.
5. **G1**: the forced buying is at the open, and the literature's sign is the
   wrong one.

Runners-up: D5 (the regime is gone), G2 (OTC, D7), A9 (pre-announced; BIZD
unshortable) and J9 (already insignificant).

## 5. What would still overturn these priors

- **The base rate is for anomalies as published.** A truly factor-neutral
  construction could lift the D-rows towards the 17% alpha-t share.
- **The IHS report is a vendor's two-year sample, and Gowri Shankar–Miller
  disagree with it.** A post-effective reversal leg of D8 is a different trade,
  not covered here.
- **Several priors are abstracts or search summaries** (Bartov–Konchitchki,
  Jeon et al., the Johnson–So magnitude). None may key a pre-registration until
  the full text is read.
