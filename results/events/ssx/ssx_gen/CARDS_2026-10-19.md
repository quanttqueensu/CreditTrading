## 1. Cards

### G11. Record-date delivery fails (the E3 test the desk can run now)

- **Mechanism.** A payment in lieu of an exempt-interest dividend is taxable to the
  lender (`docs/BRIEF.md`, the short side). Muni-CEF lenders should therefore recall
  before record dates. Failed recalls should then show up as delivery fails around
  record dates. Taxable-bond CEFs are the natural negative control: their
  distributions are ordinary income anyway, so a payment in lieu costs their lender
  nothing. Corp catalogue row E3, cited and not re-proposed.
- **Loser.** Our own shorts, through recall and buy-in. This is a risk input, not
  alpha.
- **Measured** [V; `ssx_gen_ftd.py census`].
  - Unit: name × settlement day with fails of at least 10,000 shares, in 2019–22.
  - Offset 0 is the first settlement date on or after the record date.
  - The CEF groups come from a name regex. Misassignments are visible in
    `ftd_cef_group_assignment.csv`: for example, a RiverNorth muni fund and a
    Cohen & Steers preferred fund both landed in "equity" [U].

  | group | names | record events | fail rate at offset 0 | baseline | ratio |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | muni CEF | 147 | 5,911 | 4.55% | 1.66% | **2.73** |
  | taxable-bond CEF (control) | 132 | 5,255 | 7.94% | 3.45% | **2.30** |
  | equity CEF | 306 | 9,106 | 7.69% | 2.70% | 2.85 |
  | BDC (current map) | 43 | 1,017 | 23.1% | 5.95% | 3.88 |

- **Reading.** Fails ramp into the record date and fall below baseline after it, in
  **every** group. The muni excess over the control is a ratio of ratios of **1.19**.
  This is the graveyard's second pattern: the control scores nearly as well as the
  treatment. The FTD route cannot show a muni-specific recall.
- **Caveat** [U]. A failed stock-loan return may never reach NSCC's CNS fails, so
  this does **not** kill E3 itself.
- **What survives.** Delivery fails cluster about 2.3–3.9× in the sessions just
  before record dates, across all CEFs and BDCs. That is a calendar fact for any
  short leg.
- **Kill gate for the rest of E3** (zero trials): use the desk's own `borrow_status`
  snapshots, once at least 3 months exist. If the muni flip rate in sessions
  [−3, 0] before record dates is at most 1.5× the taxable-CEF rate, close E3.
- **Clock and PIT source:** CRSP `rcrddt` (declared weeks ahead). SEC fails files
  arrive with a lag of 2–6 weeks.
- **Neighbour:** N7, N2, E3. **Counter:** none.

### G3. A pooled situations book (governance wrapper)

- **The problem.** Single situations (G2, G10, activist settlements, unusual
  tenders) never reach D7's 250 trades one at a time.
- **Design.**
  1. **Registration before entry.** A dated file in `results/events/situations/`,
     committed to git **before** the first order. It states:
     - the thesis;
     - the catalyst and its date;
     - the stated probability of the catalyst outcome, with the market-implied
       probability beside it;
     - the entry rule and the size (a fixed risk unit, the same for every
       situation);
     - the loss budget, which forces an exit;
     - the hard exit date.
  2. **Every registered situation is traded at the rule size.** No situation is
     registered and left untraded, and none is deregistered. The commit timestamp
     must precede the first broker-confirmed fill, and a script checks this.
  3. **One counter and one trial.** EVENTS, spent once, when the verdict is taken.
  4. **The verdict needs at least 30 closed situations.** It is scored two ways,
     both declared in advance:
     - (a) pooled P&L per risk unit, with a t-statistic against the EVENTS bar;
     - (b) the log score of the stated probabilities against the market-implied
       ones. (b) extracts information from far fewer events than a Sharpe ratio.
- **What would make it dishonest.**
  - registering after the move;
  - registering many and funding some;
  - editing an exit date or loss budget after entry;
  - dropping situations that "were not really situations";
  - sizing by conviction;
  - choosing the scoring rule after seeing outcomes.
  Each of these is the sweep-and-pick death in slow motion.
- **Compatibility.**
  - It is compatible with H14: no rule keys on a written number, because the
    market-implied probability is re-measured at entry.
  - It is compatible with `/prereg`: each registration is a pre-registration.
  - It is **incompatible** with "alpha only" unless D4 is run on the pooled book.
- **Kill gate.** If fewer than 10 situations are registered in the first 12
  months, the book cannot reach 30 within 3 years: close it.
- **Feeders already counted:**
  - proxy contests at CEFs/BDCs: 264 episodes since 2010, 131 at funds listed
    today [doc: `results/events/EDGAR_EVENTS_2026-10-12.md`];
  - G10 conversions;
  - G2.

### G1. Reg SHO threshold status, rebuilt terms-clean

- **Mechanism.** A threshold security has fails of at least 10,000 shares **and**
  at least 0.5% of shares outstanding for 5 consecutive settlement days. A
  participant whose fail in one lasts 13 consecutive settlement days must close it
  out by purchase. Both are fetched rule texts [V: 17 CFR 242.203(b)(3) and
  (c)(6), read 2026-10-19]. The loser would be the forced buyer.
- **Why it is not the dead neighbour (`short-pressure`).** Close-out is a dated
  legal clock, not crowding.
- **Why it fails anyway.**
  - Rule 204 close-outs are due "**by no later than the beginning of regular
    trading hours**" [V: 242.204(a)]. The forced buy happens before an MOC desk
    can stand on the other side.
  - FTDs show no price distortion in 1,492 NYSE stocks, 2005–08 (Fotak, Raman and
    Yadav, 2014, JFE) [S abstract].
- **Count** [V; `ftd_threshold_like_episodes_2019_2022.csv`]. Over 2019–22 (less
  the six missing half-months):
  - CEFs had **30 threshold-like episodes** across 28 names (taxable 16, equity 10,
    muni 4): about 8 a year;
  - **3** runs reached the 18-day close-out proxy;
  - BDCs had **0** episodes.
  - **None of the 30 is a deployed-13 name**, by name (PIMCO Dynamic Income
    *Strategy* is PDX, not PDI).
  - Rebuilding from the SEC file is the terms-clean route. Storing the exchanges'
    own lists is barred (`results/events/ssx/ssx_data/DATA_MAP_2026-10-19.md`).
- **Verdict.** D7 as alpha: about 8 a year against E's bar of 30 or more. The risk
  use is closed for the current book.
- **Re-open trigger.** One name: NYSE listed NMCO, a Nuveen muni CEF, on
  2026-10-16 [doc: data map, V there]. Re-open only if a C4-family fund the book
  holds short appears on a rebuilt list.
- **Counter:** none.

### G6. Muni de minimis cliff (fair-value input for muni NAVs)

- **Mechanism.** A muni bought below par minus ¼ point per full year to maturity
  has its market discount taxed as **ordinary income**. Retail demand falls off a
  kink, and market-discount munis "command higher yields" (Ang, Bhansali and Xing,
  2010, JF 65(2)) [S abstract].
- **The desk's exposure.** In a rate shock a muni-CEF NAV is matrix-priced. If the
  vendor's curve ignores the kink, the NAV overstates what holdings near the line
  would fetch. The z-score then reads a fair-value shift as a dislocation. This is
  M4 territory.
- **Loser.** The book, if it buys "cheap" munis whose NAV is the thing that is
  rich.
- **Negative control** (H12). Taxable-bond CEFs: no exemption, so no kink.
- **Kill gate** (zero trials, one query; **not run here**, by scope). On the R2
  MSRB tape (2005 → 2025-12-31 [doc: data map]), restricted to 2013–22, take
  customer sales in fixed-coupon bonds and compute the par share traded below
  100 − 0.25 × full years to maturity.
  - This treats OID bonds approximately [U].
  - **If the December 2022 share is under 10%**, the kink is too rare to bias a
    fund NAV: close.
  - Otherwise, measure the muni-CEF holdings share near the line from N-PORT.
- **Magnitude:** unknown, and not guessed.
- **Counter:** none until it changes a CEF policy, which would cost a CEF trial.

### G10. Closed-end grantor trust → ETF conversions

- **Mechanism.** OTC grantor trusts have a fixed share count and **no AP**: the
  CEF discount, outside the 1940 Act. On a dated conversion the AP mechanism
  arrives and the price converges to NAV. The loser is the holder who sells at the
  discount before the conversion.
- **Count.**
  - EDGAR [V, offline]: 19 Grayscale trusts file 10-K/10-Q, and **12 have an
    8-A12B** (4 in 2024, 5 in 2025, 3 in 2026 through Q3).
  - Alpaca [V]: the unconverted BCHG, ETCG and LTCN are tradable and **not
    shortable**; GBAT is not tradable.
  - Conversions continued into 2026, for the NEAR and AAVE trusts [S, aggregator
    news].
- **Verdict.** D7: about 4 a year, and the wave is mostly spent. The expression is
  long-only and the asset-class hedge is unclear.
- **Mandate.** Crypto may be outside "alpha only, credit/multi-asset": the team
  lead must rule.
- **Use.** G3 feeders only.
- **Kill gate.** If fewer than 3 unconverted trusts have a filed conversion route
  (S-1 or 19b-4), close.
