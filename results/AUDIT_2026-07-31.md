**31 July 2026, 16:53 ET.** Triggered by: "go through our whole process end to end,
find what's going wrong or unusual and how we should adapt."

Every number below was measured today. Reproduce with the scripts in
`scripts/audit/` (see §8). Nothing here is carried over from an earlier write-up

## 1. Headline

The CEF discount alpha is **real and survived two deliberate attempts to kill it**.
That is the strongest result the project has produced and it should be stated
without hedging.

The deployed *configuration*, however, is wrong in three separate ways, and the
operational layer underneath it is reporting fiction. The expected net Sharpe of
what is actually running is **~0.51**, not the 0.82 recorded at deployment.
Fixing the holding period alone takes it to **~0.73**.

## 2. The alpha survives every control I could build

The live book looks like a sector bet. It is **66% net short municipal CEFs and
57% net long multi-sector taxable CEFs** — not what "dollar-neutral" implies. Mean
net muni exposure across the full sample is −5.1% of gross, but the *current*
book is at −66%. So the obvious hypothesis was: this is muni-vs-taxable in
costume, exactly the way `S3-wrapper` was a credit-quality tilt in costume.

It is not. Two independent control sets, neither of which was in Test 7:

| control set | alpha %/yr | t | R² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HY/IG/rates/equity — the Test 7 set | +8.21 | +5.67 | 0.0037 |
| + MUB (IG muni ETF) | +8.44 | +5.71 | 0.0059 |
| + MUB, HYD (HY muni ETF) | +8.88 | +5.69 | 0.0117 |
| + MUB, HYD, duration spread (TLT−IEF) | +8.83 | +5.67 | 0.0125 |
| CEF market factor (EW all 44 funds) | +7.59 | +5.87 | 0.0043 |
| CEF market + taxable-minus-muni spread | +7.61 | +5.89 | 0.0049 |
| **all five CEF group factors** | **+7.63** | **+5.90** | **0.0057** |

ETF proxies are the weak control — muni *CEF* discounts do not move like MUB. The
strong control is the CEF sector's own group returns, and under it every group
beta is ≤ 0.025 and the alpha is untouched. The return is idiosyncratic to the
discount signal.

**Caveat that remains:** the −66% muni tilt is not a *return* risk per the
regression, but it is an unmonitored *concentration*. There is no group exposure
limit in the frozen spec.

## 4. The one-day execution lag, quantified

`scripts/cef/validate.py:86` uses `held = W.shift(1)`. W is built from day *t*'s
close **and** day *t*'s NAV. NAV does not publish until after that close, so
`shift(1)` implies entering at a price that did not yet exist when the signal
became computable. An MOC order decided in the evening fills at day *t+1*'s
close — `shift(2)`.

| | shift(1) | shift(2) | loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gross SR | 1.26 | 0.95 | −25% |
| CAGR | +7.66% | +5.69% | −26% |
| net SR at hold=5 | 0.82 | **0.51** | **−38%** |

This is not a routing bug and MOC is still the correct order type. It means the
0.82 recorded at deployment assumed an unobtainable entry price. **The honest
expected net Sharpe of the running configuration is 0.51.**

Decay is consistent across holds — shift(2) costs 26% at hold=1, 25% at hold=2,
21% at hold=10, 14% at hold=21. Slower configurations lose less to the lag but
lose far more to decay.

## 5. Signal structure — why breadth, not timing

| horizon | 1d | 2d | 3d | 5d | 8d | 10d | 15d | 21d | 42d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IC | 0.040 | 0.052 | 0.063 | 0.074 | 0.083 | 0.083 | 0.085 | 0.097 | 0.103 |

Raw information *rises* monotonically with horizon; IC/√h decays with a **31.1
trading-day half-life**. So the underlying signal is slow. Yet Sharpe is highest
at short holds, because IR ≈ IC·√BR — rebalancing multiplies breadth faster than
IC decays. n = 41,000–42,000 fund-days per horizon.

**This is a breadth machine, not a timing machine, and it is currently
under-trading its own breadth.**

## 7. The pattern across thirteen dead mechanisms

Causes of death cluster far more tightly than the individual write-ups suggest:

- **Six were stale-price artifacts** — `credit_rv` (bid-ask bounce), `S1` (one
  vendor prices every issuer), `single-fund-PD` (NAV staleness, sign positive),
  `leadlag` (non-synchronous trading), `E1`, `nport-trace-nav`.
- **Three lost to their own control group** — `dealer-constraint`,
  `short-pressure`, `S3-wrapper` scored no better than Treasuries.
- **Two died on arithmetic** — `pair-reversion` needed 32.8× leverage; costs do
  not diversify while volatility does.

**The detection machinery is genuinely good** — the controls work and they killed
things that looked excellent. The *generation* side keeps returning to one well:
price minus a stale mark. That well is dry in ETFs because APs arbitraged it
(188bp → 3.8bp). CEF discount works precisely because no AP mechanism exists
there. That is the generalisable lesson, not "credit RV is hard."

## 8. Where the mathematics should be pointed

The estimator is a 252-day rolling z-score. Each assumption is wrong in a fixable
way, ranked by expected value:

1. **The fair discount θ is not constant over 252 days.** A boxcar mean has a
   ~126-day effective lag and weights stale data equally. A state-space model with
   θ_t as a slowly-varying latent state separates "temporarily cheap" from
   "permanently re-rated." This is the same defect as fault #5 approached from the
   estimator side, and it is the highest-value change.
2. **Mean-reversion speed κ is assumed identical across funds.** Fit an OU process
   per fund by MLE; the implied half-life *is* that fund's optimal holding period.
   Today a 2-day-half-life fund and a 40-day one trade on the same clock.
3. **Kurtosis is 41.2 and the model is Gaussian.** `clip(-4, 4)` is a band-aid on a
   misspecified tail. Empirical quantile mapping or Student-t makes |z| mean the
   same thing across funds.
4. **The rebalance grid is a search where a closed form exists.**
   Gârleanu–Pedersen (2013) gives the optimal trade rate directly from signal decay
   and cost curvature — trade a fixed fraction toward an "aim" portfolio that
   overweights slow-decaying predictors. The hold=2 result in §3 is a coarse
   discretisation of that optimum.

**On higher frequency, honestly.** These funds trade $3–45m/day and we paid 94bp
crossing their spreads on day one. Higher-frequency *signal* is capacity-dead
here. But a CEF's NAV is fixed intraday, so intraday price movement *is* pure
discount movement — and the highest-value use of that data is **execution, not
alpha**. Modelled cost is 1.73bp; realised was 94bp. Closing that gap is worth
more Sharpe than any new signal in the queue.

## 9. Incident: order stacking during the fix, 2026-07-31 evening

Recorded because the mistake was mine and the detection method is the lesson.

**What happened.** Four successive armed runs were made while fixing the sizing
path. After each I checked `ib.openTrades()` from a NEW client id, saw zero, and
concluded nothing had transmitted. That inference was wrong: **`openTrades()` only
returns orders belonging to the querying client id.** Orders were transmitting on
every run and accumulating unseen. `reqAllOpenOrders()` finally showed **79
PreSubmitted MOC orders**, including full-book re-buys from the runs made before
`arm()` was wired — `AWF BUY 5068`, `DSL BUY 4419`, `HYT BUY 4773`. Left alone
they would have roughly doubled the book in Monday's closing auction.

**Clearing them.** Per-order `cancelOrder` failed with error 10147 (`OrderId not
found`) — IBKR will not let one client id cancel another's orders.
`reqGlobalCancel()` did work, but the connection that issued it kept showing 79
pending because `ib.trades()` served a stale local cache that never received the
confirmations. Three independent fresh connections (including master `clientId=0`)
then agreed: **0 open orders, positions unchanged, gross unchanged at $2.07M CAD.**

**Rules that follow from this:**
1. **Never verify order state with `openTrades()` from a fresh client id.** Use
   `reqAllOpenOrders()`, and prefer a second independent connection to confirm.
2. **`ib.trades()` is a client-local cache, not broker truth.** Status can be
   arbitrarily stale for orders another client owns.
3. **Cancel across clients needs `reqGlobalCancel()`** or a reconnect on the
   originating client id.
4. **Re-running an armed book is not idempotent** — each run stacks another order
   set. There is no dedupe. Cancel before re-running.

**End state:** one clean set of **16 MOC orders, $27,790 notional** (~3.5% of
gross), one per name, no duplicates, queued for Monday's close. Both scheduled
jobs remain at `DRY_RUN=1`.
