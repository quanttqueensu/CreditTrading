# Hunt 1 — across everything (2026-11-10)

The first hunt of `docs/MISSION.md` §10, Phase 2: 24 short cards, six mechanism types, five
asset classes, ranked. **No backtest was run for this file.** Where a card quotes a figure it
is copied from the named repo file `[S]`; nothing else carries a number, on purpose. Capacity
is stated in words until Explore measures it against volume.

**Data on hand** [V: read 2026-11-09 and 11-10]: the R2 mirror (CRSP daily to 2025-12-31,
Compustat, IBES, Thomson insiders and 13F, SDC, TRACE: `results/events/ssx/ssx_data/R2_FULL_INVENTORY_2026-10-19.md`);
the laptop panels (`results/mission/PHASE0_2026-11-09.md` §2, all stale by one to seven
months); Alpaca's data API for current prices and auction prints.

**How the ranking works.** Four judgements, each 1 to 5, multiplied: mechanism (is someone
forced or constrained, on a knowable date), independence from the CEF discount sleeves,
capacity at $1–10M, and ease of testing with data we hold. They are my judgement, not
measurements.

## The cards

**1. `month_end_ief`** — written and on shadow: `results/ideas/2026-11-10_month_end_treasury.md`.

**2. `ust_auction_cycle` — the Treasury auction concession.**
*Mechanism:* dealers must absorb each coupon auction, so they cheapen the sector beforehand
and it recovers afterwards. *Counterparty:* primary dealers hedging supply they are obliged
to bid for. *Persistence:* the size is small per auction and the dates are public, yet the
supply still has to be warehoused. *Trade:* short the matching-maturity Treasury ETF into
the auction, long after it. *Data:* the auction calendar already on the laptop
(`data/auction/auction_calendar.parquet`, from Treasury's FiscalData) and IEF, TLT, SHY
returns. *Capacity:* liquid ETFs and futures. *Correlation:* with `month_end_ief` on dates
where the two overlap; otherwise none expected. *Lookahead trap:* use the announcement date,
not the auction date, to know an auction is coming.

**3. `pead_wide` — post-earnings-announcement drift on a wide universe.**
*Mechanism:* investors under-react to earnings surprises, so prices keep drifting the same
way for weeks. *Counterparty:* holders who anchor on the old estimate, and index funds that
do not trade on news. *Persistence:* weaker in large caps and after publication `[U]`, which
is why the test is by size bucket. *Trade:* long the top surprise decile, short the bottom,
from the second close after the announcement, held 20 to 60 sessions. *Data:* IBES actuals
and estimates with announcement dates, CRSP returns, both on R2. *Capacity:* large in the
top 1,500 names. *Correlation:* none expected with credit CEFs. *Lookahead trap:* the
announcement timestamp (after the close means the next session), and estimates as they stood
before the announcement.

**4. `st_reversal_wide` — short-term reversal, within industry.**
*Mechanism:* a stock pushed by uninformed order flow reverts once the pressure ends; the
reversal is the wage for providing liquidity. *Counterparty:* whoever needed to trade in a
hurry. *Persistence:* it is paid for bearing inventory risk, and it is largest when
volatility is high. *Trade:* each day, long last week's losers and short its winners within
industry, skipping the most recent day to avoid bid-ask bounce. *Data:* CRSP daily on R2.
*Capacity:* depends on the size cut, which Explore must show. *Correlation:* low with the
CEF sleeves, although both are reversal in kind. *Note:* the BDC screen found exactly this
in liquid BDCs `[S: results/IDEA_LEDGER.md]`. *Lookahead trap:* the skip day, and delisting
returns.

**5. `turn_of_month_eq` — equity turn of the month.**
*Mechanism:* salaries, pension contributions and fund subscriptions arrive around month end
and are invested within days. *Counterparty:* nobody loses knowingly; the flow is not
price-sensitive. *Trade:* long the equity index from the last session of a month through
the first three of the next, flat otherwise. *Data:* SPY in the ETF panel; CRSP index
returns on R2 for a long history. *Capacity:* index futures and SPY. *Correlation:* its
window touches `month_end_ief`'s last day; the instruments differ. The earlier look at this
on the CEF book was parked for a test anchored on pay dates `[S: docs/SYSTEM.md §5,
2026-11-02]`; this is the index version.

**6. `cef_exdate_drift` — CEFs keep falling after the ex-date.**
*Mechanism:* income buyers hold for the distribution and sell after it, and the price drops
by more than the distribution. *Counterparty:* retail holders who buy for the payout date.
*Result on file:* cumulative abnormal return over the three sessions after the ex-date of
−34.3bp (t −14.9) on C4's families and −20.5bp (t −6.8) on the 17 credit funds, 2005–22
`[S: docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md, SCR-C4-EXDATE]`. *Trade:* short at the ex-date close, cover
three sessions later, hedged with the fund's peers. *Data:* the CEF family panels and
distributions; ex-dates are announced weeks ahead. *Capacity:* small per fund, several
hundred events a year. *Correlation:* some with C4, which earns part of its P&L in the same
window. *Why revive:* it was approved and built, then left off because it lowered the return
on capital of a two-account book; as a shadow book it costs nothing to watch.

**7. `earn_announce_premium` — the earnings announcement premium.**
*Mechanism:* stocks earn more in the days around a scheduled announcement, as attention and
announcement risk both rise. *Counterparty:* holders who sell ahead of the uncertainty.
*Trade:* long stocks expected to report in the next few sessions, short those that are not.
*Data:* IBES announcement dates on R2; the expected date must come from the previous year's
pattern. *Capacity:* large. *Correlation:* none expected. *Lookahead trap:* using the
realised date instead of a date predicted from history.

**8. `insider_clusters` — several insiders buying at once.**
*Mechanism:* insiders buy with their own money when they think the price is wrong, and a
cluster is harder to explain away than one purchase. *Counterparty:* the sellers on the
other side of an under-followed name. *Count on file:* 3,485 liquid clusters over 2010–22
`[S: docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md, SSX-1]`; never tested for returns. *Data:* Thomson insiders on
R2 (its 2026 rows are incomplete); the filing date is the knowable date. *Capacity:* mid
and small caps, so it must be shown against volume. *Correlation:* none expected.

**9. `vix_term_structure` — selling volatility only when the curve pays for it.**
*Mechanism:* investors overpay for protection, so implied volatility exceeds realised on
average; the premium is collected safely only while the curve slopes upward. *Counterparty:*
hedgers, who pay for insurance by mandate. *Trade:* short a front VIX futures proxy when the
curve is in contango, flat or long when it inverts. *Data:* Cboe indices in the repo
(`data/feeds/vol_indices_daily.parquet`, stale to 2026-07-30) and the volatility ETPs.
*Capacity:* fine at this size. *Correlation:* positive with credit in a crash, which is the
reason to size it small. *History:* 5 February 2018 is the case to replay.

**10. `cef_taxloss_universe`** — December tax-loss selling and the January rebound, across
every CEF on CRSP rather than 13. Half the core's signal already sits on December and
January dates `[S: docs/SYSTEM.md §2]`; with a few hundred funds a season has breadth.

**11. `trend_futures`** — time-series trend on 30 to 50 futures. The 14-ETF version earned
Sharpe 0.45 `[S: ledger, XASSET-X4]`; the argument for a retest is breadth, and it needs a
free continuous-futures build first.

**12. `overnight_intraday`** — index returns accrue overnight and little intraday; a
close-to-open holder against an open-to-close holder. Needs opening-auction prints, which
Alpaca serves.

**13. `fallen_angel_stress`** — bonds downgraded out of investment grade are sold by
mandate and recover: −424bp trough, t −17.5, 82–85% reversal `[S: ledger, S3-forced-flow]`.
Fires in stress only; ANGL or FALN against HYG, switched by the market-state layer.

**14. `index_adds_midsmall`** — S&P 400 and 600 additions and deletions; passes the event
census's count gate `[S: SSX-1]`, sources disagree on dates.

**15. `pcef_recon`** — the PCEF index's quarterly changes: +184bp then −120bp `[S: ledger]`.
Needs announcement dates; Invesco gave written consent for the holdings `[S: docs/SYSTEM.md §5]`.

**16. `short_interest_xs`** — heavily shorted stocks underperform; days-to-cover as the
measure. Short-interest data on R2 must be checked for its publication lag.

**17. `crowding_13f`** — names crowded with hedge funds fall hardest when funds deleverage.
13F is filed 45 days after quarter end, which is the knowable date.

**18. `low_beta`** — investors who cannot lever overpay for high-beta stocks. A slow, large
premium; a risk-premium sleeve, which the 2026-11-09 ruling now allows.

**19. `cef_fast_z`** — the core's signal on a 63-day window: gross Sharpe 1.75 against the
252-day book's lower figure `[S: ledger]`, killed on net. Same family as the core.

**20. `open_auction_fill`** — fill the CEF books at the next open instead of the close:
+293bp a year on the fit, +313.6bp on the holdout `[S: ledger, C5]`. An execution change to
C4 and the core, to test on paper.

**21. `levered_etf_close`** — leveraged and inverse ETFs must rebalance in the last
minutes of the day in the direction of the day's move. Needs intraday bars.

**22. `dealer_gamma_intraday`** — when dealers are long gamma they damp intraday moves, and
when short they amplify them. SPY and QQQ option marks are on the laptop to 2026-07.

**23. `pff_index_adds`** — preferred-stock index additions: 101 to 143 a year in 2020–21 at
a median of two days of volume `[S: SSX-1, CR15]`.

**24. `quarter_end_funding`** — banks shrink balance sheets at quarter end and funding
markets tighten. Real, but the instrument that expresses it at this size is not obvious.
